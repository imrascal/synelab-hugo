Congrats to Yuanyuan, Bin and Fei for their co-first authored paper "[Boltzmann Sigmoidal Modulation of Vanadium Doping in Monolayer MoS2 by Potassium Iodide](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.nanolett.6c03019)" being published in Nano Letters!

Substitutional doping underpins semiconductor electronics, but the atomic thinness of two-dimensional (2D) semiconductors rules out ion implantation, leaving in situ doping during growth as the practical route — and its governing mechanism has remained elusive. Using V-doped monolayer MoS2 as a model system, this work established a STEM-calibrated Raman probe (defect-activated double-resonance modes at 100–250 cm−1) to quantify the real dopant concentration, and found that the doping density saturates far below the precursor feeding ratio (~9% plateau despite up to 80% nominal feeding). The saturation level is tuned by the potassium iodide (KI) promoter following a Boltzmann sigmoidal law from ~4% to ~12%, which first-principles calculations reconcile with the edge passivation substitution model: iodine termination lowers the V incorporation barrier more strongly than that of Mo, activating heteroatom substitution. This iodide-activated doping paradigm advances doping theory for low-dimensional systems and charts a generalizable route to controlled heteroatom substitution in 2D semiconductors for electronic, spintronic and catalytic applications.
